Taste it
slowly.

Every honey has its own colour, scent and story. This guide shows you how to taste like a beekeeper, and how to share it with the people at your table.

The ritual

Five steps, one spoon.

Pour a little of each honey into small bowls, keep a glass of water close, and go in order from lightest to darkest.

STEP 1 OF 5
Look
STEP 01 · LOOK

Hold it to the light.

Lift a spoon toward a window. Notice the colour, from pale straw to deep mahogany, and how clear or cloudy it is. Tiny bubbles and specks of pollen mean it hasn’t been filtered to death.

TRYLine the bowls up from lightest to darkest. You are reading the season the bees worked in.
STEP 02 · SMELL

Breathe it in.

Warm the spoon in your hand for a moment, then bring it close. Short sniffs first, then one slow breath. Is it flowery, fruity, warm like caramel, or earthy like wood after rain?

TRYClose your eyes. The first word that comes to mind is usually right.
STEP 03 · TASTE

Let it melt.

Place a little on your tongue and don’t swallow straight away. Let it spread on its own. Sweetness arrives first, then the flavours of the flower, and sometimes a little acidity.

STEP 04 · FEEL

Notice the texture.

Press it against the roof of your mouth. Is it thin and silky, thick and buttery, or slightly grainy? Crystals are a good sign. They mean the honey is raw.

TRYRub a drop between finger and thumb. Raw honey feels slightly tacky, never slippery.
STEP 05 · FINISH

Wait for the end.

After you swallow, notice what stays. Light honeys vanish quickly. Darker ones linger with notes of malt or herbs. Take a sip of water before the next bowl.

SHAREAsk everyone at the table for one word. You’ll be surprised how different they are.
